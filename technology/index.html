Technology

Built on what works, ready for what comes next.

Four horizons

Every technology carries a readiness label.

TRL is the Technology Readiness Level, from 1 (idea) to 9 (proven at scale). The rule for each horizon decides when, or whether, we use it.

  1. TRL 8–9

    Proven

    Build with it from day one: mass timber, silicon solar, LFP batteries, heat pumps, greenhouses.

  2. TRL 6–8

    Scaling

    Design for it, buy it in Stage 5 (2028–2031) if bankable: tandem solar, sodium-ion, AI controls.

  3. TRL 4–6

    Emerging

    Leave space and interfaces, adopt in Phase 2: transparent wood, bio-materials, self-healing concrete.

  4. TRL 1–3

    Frontier

    Study openly with independent partners, never depend on it: coherence, resonance, subtle energy.

City systems

Five systems keep the city alive.

Energy

Solar microgrid & storage

Proven · TRL 8–9

Ground-mounted arrays, solar glass on the domes and panels on the spire feed a community-owned microgrid. Batteries cover the night; the regional grid and renewable gensets (biogas or bio-ethanol, never diesel) cover long cloudy spells. Micro-hydro and geothermal heat join where the chosen land has them.

Sodium-ion is the preferred storage chemistry if 2026–2027 pricing holds. Multi-day iron-air storage becomes relevant when Phase 2 loads justify it.

Generation
2–5 MWp solar, incl. spire and domes
Storage
8–20 MWh, sodium-ion or LFP
Resilience
Grid-tied, can island; biogas or bio-ethanol backup
Later
Iron-air, geothermal in Phase 2

Water

Closed-loop water

Proven · TRL 8–9

Rain is captured from dome and roof surfaces, stored and filtered. Wastewater passes through planted treatment systems and wetlands, then returns as irrigation for the food forest.

Each step is proven. Sizing depends on the site's rainfall, which is why site selection in Stage 1 matters.

Supply
Rainwater harvesting + well/municipal
Treatment
Living machines, constructed wetlands
Reuse
Greywater to irrigation
Monitoring
Real-time quality sensors

Food

Greenhouses, food forest & aquaponics

Proven · TRL 8–9

Solar greenhouses and layered agroforestry use a fraction of the energy of indoor farms. Aquaponics adds fish and greens in one loop. Fully indoor growing is kept for niche crops only.

Phase 1
1–2 ha agroforestry + greenhouses
Phase 2
5–10 ha, near self-sufficiency
Indoor
Herbs, microgreens, mushrooms
Loop
Kitchen compost back to soil

Buildings

Mass timber & bio-based materials

Proven to Emerging

Factory-made timber panels go up fast and store carbon. The tallest timber building today is 86.6 m, so a 60–120 m timber-steel spire sits within the demonstrated range, though its exact form is new.

3D-printed construction handles foundations, garden walls and studios; prefabricated CLT remains the main method for the domes.

Structure
CLT and glulam, prefabricated
Envelope
Hempcrete, mycelium insulation
Glazing
Solar glass; tandem PV if durable
Site work
3D-printed walls and foundations

Wellbeing

Healthy interiors & open research

Proven + Frontier

Daylight, clean air, quiet and greenery are designed in from the start. Optional neurotech tools support meditation practice and research, and are never required.

Questions such as group coherence or the effect of sacred proportions are studied openly with independent partners. Results are published, including null results.

Lighting
Circadian, tuned by time of day
Air
CO₂, humidity, VOC sensors
Tools
Optional EEG/fNIRS for practice
Research
Preregistered, published, incl. nulls

Scaling in 2026

What changed this year.

These technologies moved from labs into factories in 2026. Light City will decide between them with field data from 2027–2028.

Energy

Perovskite-silicon tandem solar

Commercial production began in the US with modules above 27% efficiency, delivering in 2027. Candidate for the spire canopy and dome glazing.

Storage

Sodium-ion batteries

Mass production by end of 2026, safe and cold-tolerant, with costs approaching LFP. Preferred Phase 1 storage if pricing holds.

Realism notes

Lessons already learned.

  • Vertical farming is not the default. Several well-funded vertical farms closed between 2023 and 2026, and fully indoor farms can use several times the energy of greenhouses.
  • Timelines slip. Solid-state batteries are years behind early promises. We plan with what ships today and treat new technology as an upgrade path.
  • The spire is the boldest structure. At TRL 4–5 it is a novel assembly of proven parts, so it gets the most engineering review in Stage 2.
  • No dependence on the frontier. Nothing residents rely on for safety, comfort or cost depends on unproven technology.
