Technology Catalog

Modern and emerging technologies available for Light City implementation

Overview​

This catalog identifies currently available and near-term emerging technologies that can be integrated into Light City's design. Each technology is assessed for:

  • Availability: Commercial, pilot, or R&D stage
  • TRL (Technology Readiness Level): 1-9 scale
  • Cost: Relative cost indicator
  • Light City Application: Specific use cases

Structural Systems​

Cross-Laminated Timber (CLT)​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $$ (Medium)

Description:

  • Engineered wood panels (3-12 layers) laminated crosswise
  • Strength comparable to concrete, lighter weight
  • Carbon sequestration (stores ~1 ton CO₂ per m³)
  • Fire-resistant (chars on outside, maintains structural integrity)

Manufacturers:

  • Structurlam (Canada/US)
  • KLH (Austria)
  • Binderholz (Austria)
  • Nordic Structures (Canada)

Light City Applications:

  • Communosphere dome frames
  • Temple structures
  • Multi-story residential buildings
  • Spire tower hybrid (CLT + steel)

Specifications:

  • Spans: Up to 20m without intermediate support
  • Heights: Proven to 18 stories (Mjøstårnet, Norway - 85.4m)
  • Seismic performance: Excellent (ductile connections)
  • Acoustic: Good sound insulation with proper detailing

Geodesic Dome Systems​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 7-8)
Cost: $$ (Medium to High)

Description:

  • Spherical structures based on icosahedron or similar polyhedra
  • Extremely strong (distributes stress evenly)
  • Minimal materials for enclosed volume
  • Sacred geometry inherent in design

Suppliers:

  • Pacific Domes (US) - up to 50m diameter
  • Timberline Geodesics (US)
  • Natural Spaces Domes (US)
  • Geometrica (Mexico) - large-span steel domes

Light City Applications:

  • Communosphere primary structure
  • Temple domes
  • Greenhouse/garden enclosures
  • Event pavilions

Specifications:

  • Diameters: 10-60m readily available
  • Materials: Steel, aluminum, timber, or hybrid
  • Glazing: ETFE, polycarbonate, or glass
  • Snow/wind loads: Engineered for extreme conditions

Tensile Fabric Structures​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $ to $$ (Low to Medium)

Description:

  • PTFE-coated fiberglass or PVC-coated polyester
  • Lightweight, translucent, long-lasting (25+ years)
  • Organic, flowing forms
  • Natural light transmission

Suppliers:

  • Birdair (US)
  • Tensotherm (Germany)
  • Architen Landrell (UK)
  • FTL Design Engineering (US)

Light City Applications:

  • Plaza shade structures
  • Amphitheater canopies
  • Garden pavilions
  • Temporary/seasonal spaces

3D-Printed Concrete​

Status: Pilot/Early Commercial (TRL 6-7)
Cost: $ to $$ (Decreasing rapidly)

Description:

  • Robotic extrusion of concrete layer-by-layer
  • Complex geometries without formwork
  • 60-80% less material waste
  • Sacred geometry patterns easily integrated

Companies:

  • ICON (US) - homes and larger structures
  • Apis Cor (US/Russia)
  • COBOD (Denmark)
  • Mighty Buildings (US) - with stone composite

Light City Applications:

  • Foundation elements
  • Retaining walls with sacred geometry patterns
  • Sculptural elements
  • Affordable housing units

Current Capabilities:

  • Building size: Up to 3,000 sq ft per structure
  • Print speed: ~100-200 sq ft per day
  • Materials: Standard concrete, fiber-reinforced, stone composite

Energy Systems​

1 Building-Integrated Photovoltaics (BIPV)​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $$$ (High, but decreasing)

Description:

  • Solar cells integrated into building envelope (glazing, facades, roofing)
  • Dual function: Power generation + weather protection
  • Aesthetic options: Transparent, colored, patterned

Manufacturers:

  • Onyx Solar (Spain) - glass with integrated PV
  • Tesla Solar Roof (US)
  • Solaria (US) - high-efficiency panels
  • Heliatek (Germany) - organic PV films

Light City Applications:

  • Communosphere glazing (semi-transparent BIPV)
  • Spire tower facade
  • Temple skylights
  • Walkway canopies

Performance:

  • Efficiency: 10-20% (lower than rooftop, but dual-function)
  • Transparency: 10-40% (adjustable)
  • Lifespan: 25-30 years
  • Cost: $300-$600/m² installed

2 Flow Batteries (Long-Duration Storage)​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 7-8)
Cost: $$ (Medium, competitive for 4+ hour storage)

Description:

  • Liquid electrolytes stored in tanks
  • Scalable capacity (just add more tanks)
  • 20+ year lifespan, unlimited cycles
  • Non-flammable, recyclable

Manufacturers:

  • ESS Inc. (US) - iron flow batteries
  • Invinity Energy Systems (UK/Canada) - vanadium
  • Sumitomo Electric (Japan) - vanadium
  • Largo Clean Energy (Canada) - vanadium

Light City Applications:

  • Primary energy storage (8-80 MWh)
  • Day-to-night load shifting
  • Seasonal storage (with large enough tanks)
  • Grid services (if interconnected)

Specifications:

  • Round-trip efficiency: 65-75%
  • Discharge duration: 4-12+ hours
  • Degradation: Minimal (<1% over 20 years)
  • Cost: $300-$500/kWh (decreasing)

3 Micro-Hydro Turbines​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 9)
Cost: $ to $$ (Low to Medium)

Description:

  • Small-scale hydroelectric (1-100 kW)
  • Requires stream with consistent flow
  • 24/7 baseload power (if water available)
  • Minimal environmental impact (run-of-river)

Suppliers:

  • Canyon Hydro (US)
  • Energy Systems & Design (Canada)
  • Turbulent (Belgium) - vortex turbines
  • Natel Energy (US) - fish-safe turbines

Light City Applications:

  • Supplemental baseload (if site has water)
  • Water feature integration (aesthetic + functional)
  • Backup power system

Requirements:

  • Flow: 50+ gallons/minute minimum
  • Head: 10+ feet (3m) minimum
  • Permits: Water rights, fish passage (if applicable)

4 Solid-State Batteries​

Status: Pilot/Near-Commercial (TRL 6-7)
Cost: $$$ (High, but expected to drop)

Description:

  • Solid electrolyte instead of liquid
  • Higher energy density, safer (non-flammable)
  • Longer lifespan, faster charging
  • Expected commercial availability: 2025-2027

Companies:

  • QuantumScape (US) - backed by VW
  • Solid Power (US) - backed by BMW/Ford
  • Samsung SDI (Korea)
  • Toyota (Japan) - targeting 2027-2028

Light City Applications:

  • Phase 2-3 energy storage upgrade
  • Electric vehicle fleet
  • Portable/mobile power systems

Projected Performance:

  • Energy density: 2-3x lithium-ion
  • Lifespan: 1,000-2,000 cycles → 3,000-5,000 cycles
  • Safety: Eliminates thermal runaway risk

Water & Waste Systems​

1 Living Machines (Biological Wastewater Treatment)​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $$ (Medium)

Description:

  • Engineered wetlands with plants, bacteria, algae
  • Treats wastewater to tertiary standards
  • Beautiful, educational, odorless
  • Produces clean water for irrigation

Providers:

  • Worrell Water Technologies (US) - Living Machine®
  • Biolytix (Australia) - BioPod systems
  • Natural Systems International (US)

Light City Applications:

  • Primary wastewater treatment
  • Greywater recycling
  • Stormwater treatment
  • Educational demonstration

Specifications:

  • Capacity: 1,000-100,000+ gallons/day
  • Footprint: ~100-200 sq ft per 1,000 gal/day
  • Treatment level: BOD <10 mg/L, TSS <10 mg/L
  • Maintenance: Low (weekly monitoring)

2 Atmospheric Water Generators (AWG)​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 7-8)
Cost: $$ to $$$ (Medium to High)

Description:

  • Extract water from air humidity
  • Useful in areas with limited groundwater
  • Solar-powered models available
  • Produces potable water

Manufacturers:

  • SOURCE Global (US) - solar hydropanels
  • Watergen (Israel) - large-scale units
  • Zero Mass Water (US) - residential systems
  • Akvo (US) - atmospheric water harvesters

Light City Applications:

  • Supplemental water source
  • Emergency/backup water supply
  • Demonstration of advanced technology

Performance:

  • Production: 2-5 liters/day per m² (solar panels)
  • Humidity requirement: >30% RH optimal
  • Energy: 0.2-0.5 kWh per liter (electric models)
  • Cost: $2,000-$5,000 per kW production capacity

3 Composting Toilet Systems​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 9)
Cost: $ (Low to Medium)

Description:

  • Waterless or micro-flush toilets
  • Converts waste to compost
  • Zero sewage discharge
  • Odorless with proper ventilation

Manufacturers:

  • Clivus Multrum (US) - large commercial systems
  • Sun-Mar (Canada) - residential
  • Nature's Head (US) - compact units
  • Phoenix Composting (US) - commercial

Light City Applications:

  • All residential units (zero sewage)
  • Public restrooms
  • Produces compost for gardens
  • Eliminates water waste

Specifications:

  • Capacity: 1-500+ users per system
  • Processing time: 6-24 months to finished compost
  • Maintenance: Weekly (add bulking agent), annual (harvest)
  • Regulations: Approved in most US states with proper design

4 Rainwater Harvesting Systems​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 9)
Cost: $ (Low)

Description:

  • Collect, filter, store rainwater from roofs
  • Reduces municipal water demand 40-80%
  • Simple, proven technology
  • Scalable from residential to community

Suppliers:

  • Rain Harvesting (Australia/US)
  • Rainwater Management Solutions (US)
  • Innovative Water Solutions (US)
  • Tank manufacturers: Poly-Mart, Norwesco, etc.

Light City Applications:

  • Primary non-potable water source
  • Irrigation for gardens
  • Toilet flushing (if not composting)
  • Fire suppression reserve

Design Parameters:

  • Collection: 0.6 gallons per sq ft roof per inch rain
  • Storage: 30-90 days supply typical
  • Filtration: First-flush diverter + 5-20 micron filter
  • Cost: $2-$5 per gallon storage capacity

Food Production​

1 Vertical Hydroponic/Aeroponic Systems​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $$ to $$$ (Medium to High)

Description:

  • Soil-less growing in stacked layers
  • 90-95% less water than soil farming
  • Year-round production, climate-controlled
  • 10-20x yield per sq ft vs. traditional

Companies:

  • AeroFarms (US) - large-scale aeroponic
  • Plenty (US) - vertical farms
  • Freight Farms (US) - shipping container farms
  • ZipGrow (US) - vertical tower systems

Light City Applications:

  • Hanging gardens in communospheres
  • Dedicated vertical farm buildings
  • Supplemental food production
  • Educational demonstrations

Performance:

  • Yield: 10-30 lbs per sq ft per year (leafy greens)
  • Water: 1-5% of traditional agriculture
  • Energy: LED grow lights (30-50 W per sq ft)
  • Crops: Leafy greens, herbs, strawberries, tomatoes

2 Aquaponics Systems​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $$ (Medium)

Description:

  • Combines fish farming + hydroponics
  • Fish waste fertilizes plants
  • Plants filter water for fish
  • Closed-loop, zero-waste system

Suppliers:

  • Nelson & Pade (US) - commercial systems
  • Pentair Aquatic Eco-Systems (US)
  • The Aquaponic Source (US)
  • Backyard Aquaponics (Australia)

Light City Applications:

  • Integrated food production (fish + vegetables)
  • Educational/demonstration systems
  • Community-scale food security
  • Aesthetic water features

Specifications:

  • Fish: Tilapia, trout, catfish, bass
  • Plants: Leafy greens, herbs, fruiting vegetables
  • Ratio: 1 lb fish per 5-10 sq ft grow bed
  • Maintenance: Daily feeding, weekly water testing

3 Agroforestry & Food Forests​

Status: Proven Traditional (TRL 9)
Cost: $ (Low)

Description:

  • Multi-layer perennial food systems
  • Trees, shrubs, herbs, ground covers, root crops
  • Mimics natural forest ecosystems
  • Low maintenance once established (3-5 years)

Design Resources:

  • Permaculture Research Institute
  • Savanna Institute (US)
  • Regenerative Agriculture Foundation
  • Local permaculture consultants

Light City Applications:

  • Primary food production strategy
  • Carbon sequestration
  • Wildlife habitat
  • Beauty and biodiversity

Design Layers:

  1. Canopy: Nut/fruit trees (chestnuts, apples, pears)
  2. Understory: Smaller fruit trees (plums, cherries)
  3. Shrub: Berries (blueberries, currants, elderberries)
  4. Herbaceous: Perennial vegetables, herbs
  5. Ground cover: Strawberries, low herbs
  6. Root: Perennial root crops (sunchokes, groundnuts)
  7. Vertical: Vines (grapes, kiwi, hops)

4 Mycelium/Mushroom Cultivation​

Status: Commercially Available (TRL 8-9)
Cost: $ (Low)

Description:

  • Mushroom farming on agricultural waste
  • High protein, medicinal varieties
  • Mycelium for materials (packaging, insulation)
  • Breaks down toxins, remediates soil

Suppliers:

  • Fungi Perfecti (US) - Paul Stamets' company

Light City Applications:
