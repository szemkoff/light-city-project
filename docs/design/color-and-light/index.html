Color and Light

Palette and lighting rules for the website, diagrams, photos and the built city

Modern Organic light: daylight through glass, warm shielded lamps, clay and timber surfaces.

Palette​

The palette matches the website. Diagrams use the same colors, so documents, site and print look like one family.

Core interface colors​

NameHexUse
Pearl#F6F1E7Page surfaces, diagram backgrounds
Card#FBF8F2Cards, panels
Ink#1D1A15Text, lines
Muted#5F584CSecondary text, captions
Gold#8A5F17Links, accents, structure lines in diagrams
Gold light#E9B85AAccent on dark backgrounds
Glow#F6D38CHighlights, "current" markers
Night#0C0E15Dark sections, night scenes

Nature accents (diagrams and illustrations)​

NameHexUse
Sage#6E8B6AFood, nature, homes
Terracotta#B5643CPeople, warmth, earth, reference lines
Clay#D9C3A5Earth materials, plaster
Sky glass#9DB7C4Domes, glazing, water, air

Use light tints (about 15–30% opacity) of the nature accents for panel fills. Keep text on tints in Ink.

Lighting principles​

  • Daylight first. Domes and studios are planned for daylight through glazing and an oculus; artificial light fills in.
  • Warm and circadian. Evening light is warm (2,200–3,000 K) and dimmable; daytime light can be cooler. Integrated LED lines sit in timber ribs and coves, not bare fixtures.
  • Fire stays outdoors. No open fire indoors: it is a health, fire-code and fuel problem. Indoor gathering spaces use daylight, warm lamps and water. Outdoor fire pits appear occasionally, small and modest.
  • Crystal as accent. Quartz and glass catch and split light in small doses: a centerpiece, a window, a lamp. Never a whole wall of crystal.
  • Crystals are occasional. Most images show none. Where one appears (the temple, a home shelf), show natural quartz as one, two or three vertical points, set with intention into the ground on a stone or earth base, or on a simple pedestal or stand. No clusters shooting in every direction and no glowing, game-style crystals.
  • Dark skies. Exterior light points down, is shielded and is warm, so stars stay visible.
  • Spire light. The spire crown can carry programmable light for gatherings; it stays off or low on ordinary nights and never shines upward.
  • Protect the night. Outdoor lights are shielded, aimed down, 3000 K or warmer and dimmed when not needed, following DarkSky guidance. No glowing ground orbs, rows of candles or uplit domes. See Light on the land.

Usage guidance​

  • Text is Ink or Muted on Pearl or Card. On Night, use #F3EEE4 text and Gold light accents.
  • Gold marks links, structure lines and the most important item in a diagram. Use it sparingly.
  • Terracotta is for people, warmth and reference lines (for example the Mjøstårnet height line).
  • Check contrast: body text needs at least 4.5:1, large titles 3:1.
