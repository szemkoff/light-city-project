Architecture

This document details the architectural vision of Light City across physical, energetic, and symbolic dimensions.

Overview​

Light City's architecture serves multiple purposes:

  • Functional: Practical spaces for living and gathering
  • Energetic: Structures that elevate consciousness
  • Symbolic: Forms that communicate principles
  • Aesthetic: Beauty that inspires and uplifts

Core Design Principles​

1. Sacred Geometry​

All design is based on sacred geometric principles:

  • Circle: Unity, wholeness, infinity
  • Golden Ratio: Harmony, beauty, natural proportion
  • Platonic Solids: Fundamental forms
  • Flower of Life: Creation pattern
  • Merkaba: Energy field geometry

Application: City layout, building proportions, decorative elements

2. Light as Primary Element​

Light is not decoration but foundational:

  • Natural Light: Maximum solar integration
  • Crystalline Refraction: Structures that split light into rainbows
  • Luminescence: Materials that glow or transmit light
  • Light Paths: Designed movement of light through space

Materials: Crystal, glass, translucent stone, reflective metals

3. Sound and Resonance​

Architecture designed for harmonic resonance:

  • Acoustic Properties: Spaces amplify beneficial frequencies
  • Musical Ratios: Proportions based on harmonic intervals
  • Resonant Chambers: Specific spaces for sound healing
  • Natural Hum: The city itself maintains a harmonic tone

4. Vertical and Horizontal Integration​

Connection across dimensions:

  • Vertical: Earth to sky, roots to cosmos
  • Horizontal: Center to periphery, inner to outer
  • Central Axis: Grounding and ascending simultaneously

Phased Development & Scale​

The city grows in three phases from one radial plan. It is one interactive 3D model: every structure is tagged with the phase that builds it, and the phase switch turns on what each later phase adds (Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3). The spire, plaza, rings and 12 radial paths stay where they are; later phases fill the empty dome plots, extend the same spire and add rings outward.

Rule: build once, grow outward. Nothing built in one phase is torn down in the next. Every phase has to work as a finished place on its own, in case the next one never comes.

  • Reserved plots. Dome plots, pavilion sites and home plots are fixed in Phase 1. Until they are built they hold meadow and a tree nursery, whose young trees are replanted elsewhere. Long-lived orchards and food forest go only in the permanent belts.
  • Fill the gaps, then go outward. Ring 4 has 60 fixed home plots; Phase 1 builds 30 and later phases fill the gaps. New neighborhoods are added as new rings outside the existing ones, never on top of them.
  • Every system grows with the town. Water, sewage, food, care, learning and play are sized for each phase and built close to its people, then extended beside what is there. See How the city grows.
  • Belts are permanent. The Phase 2 food and energy belt stays as a green belt between the village and the town; Phase 3 adds its own belt further out.
  • One spire, built in sections. The foundation, basement and lift core are sized for the final height in Phase 1. Later phases add glass sections on top and lift the temporary point, then add the wind crown in Phase 3, the way the Shukhov Tower in Moscow was built from stacked sections lifted into place.
  • Only movable things move. Solar panels, greenhouses and nursery trees may be relocated. Buildings, paths, mature trees and soil are not.

Top views of the three 3D models. Each panel is drawn at its own scale.

Phase 1 - Pilot Hamlet (built in Stage 5, Dec 2028 – Nov 2031):

  • Population: 200 residents target (150-300 range)
  • Footprint: 20-30 acres (8-12 hectares)
  • Structures: Central plaza, 1 temple, 3 communosphere domes, crystal spire (90 m, base built for 240 m)
  • Energy: 2-5 MWp solar PV + 8-20 MWh storage
  • Food: 1-2 ha agroforestry, supplemented by external sources
  • In the model: 90 m crystal spire (three glass sections on a base built for 240 m), one temple and two learning pavilions, three 40 m domes, 30 homes on ring 4 in three building methods (earth-sheltered, earthbag and timber with hempcrete; see Beyond the Box), solar on ring 5, reserved plots as meadow and nursery; a heart lake with a wooded island to the south-west, joined to the plaza by a short canal to a small harbour and a narrow rill that runs between the domes into the plaza pool; low hills to the north at the back; the 12 paths turn in a gentle spiral from the plaza and pass between the domes, which sit in the middle of each sector; each dome's main doors open onto paved forecourts on the ring paths, with side doors onto the radials; a small water yard just behind the hamlet to the north, a treatment garden of reed beds just downhill to the south-east and two rain cisterns at every dome (How the city grows); a mobility hub with a solar carport where the north road ends at the edge; plan radius 222 m (Planning principles)

Phase 2 - Village (mid-2030s, after Phase 1 proves out):

  • Population: 1,500 target (1,500-3,000 range)
  • Footprint: 150-300 acres (60-120 hectares)
  • Structures: Expanded plaza, 3-5 temples, 8 domes, taller spire (150 m)
  • Energy: 5-15 MWp solar + 40-80 MWh storage
  • Food: 5-10 ha food forests, approaching self-sufficiency
  • In the model: everything from Phase 1 kept; spire extended to 150 m with two more sections, six pavilions (temples and learning), eight domes (five new at 44 m on reserved plots), 54 homes on ring 4 that add 3D-printed earth to the mix, a new ring of courtyard clusters of six homes, each cluster built with one method, short curved courtyard terraces (two and three storeys of timber and glass with green or solar roofs) and glass garden halls filling the village rings between the paths, as in the concept render, and a food and energy belt of agrivoltaics, greenhouses and food forest; a soft canal ring with stone quays joins the lake, crossed by arched timber footbridges, with solar-roofed electric boats as a transport option; beyond the core the radial paths turn gently; three work hubs (a timber market hall and workshop vaults) sit about 4 minutes on foot from every home, and three mobility hubs sit on an arc road open to the south; plan radius 460 m (about 160 acres)

Phase 3 - Town (Long-term vision):

  • Population: 8,000+ residents (8,000-12,000 range)
  • Footprint: 1-3 square miles (2.6-7.8 km²)
  • Structures: Full concentric ring layout with 17 domes (11 on the ring, 6 district domes) and a 240 m spire
  • Energy: 15-50 MWp distributed renewable + storage
  • Food: 20-50 ha regenerative agriculture
  • In the model: the village and its food and energy belt kept as built, the belt now a green belt; spire extended to 240 m with the wind crown and glass point, eight pavilions, all eleven ring domes (the twelfth sector is left to the heart rill and a water garden) and 60 ring homes; the ring-5 solar arrays give way to solar-roofed terraces, and more terraces and garden halls fill the town rings between the crescents; then outside the green belt crescents of flowing two-to-three storey terraces (curved glass facades, thin-shell concrete floor and balcony bands, timber roof edges, green roofs with skylights and living-wall ends), six 80 m district domes (each with a small temple and market green) and a new outer food and energy belt; an outer canal ring and three S-curved canals through the town link both rings, the lake and a slow river that bends round the south of the town (the city sits inside its curve), so electric boats and water buses reach every district; each district has a market hall and a row of workshops, cars stop at mobility hubs on an outer ring road, and the radial paths keep a gentle twist; plan radius 1,000 m (about 1.2 square miles). This is schematic, not a final master plan: the course of the river, lake and canals will follow the site's real landform and drainage.

The Central Spire​

The Crystal Point spire in Phase 3, 240 m. Concept rendering made on top of the 3D model; see it in 3D.

The spire is shaped like a natural quartz crystal: twelve sides, 61 m across at the foot, narrowing like a gentle cone to 44 m at 150 m, then curving in at the shoulders to 22 m at 204 m, where a blunt 26 m faceted glass point closes it at 230 m and its mast reaches 240 m. The skin is one continuous white steel diagrid of tall staggered diamonds, about 16 m wide and 50 m tall at the foot, and each diamond is cut into four facets of dichroic solar glass like a cut stone: blue and violet above, warm amber and clear below. A ring of slender mullions marks the observatory at 72–90 m, where a mirrored globe room hangs round the core. Higher up the glass climbs on a slant into the open lattice of the wind crown, higher on one side than the other, so the turbines show through the open diamonds. By day the facets throw rainbow glints; at night it glows softly from inside, with a small inner crystal lit in the point.

Section and plans. The crystal is built in sections of 30 m: 90 m in Phase 1, 150 m in Phase 2 and 240 m in Phase 3.

In the source (visionary reference)​

The source describes a 40,000 ft (about 12 km) crystal spire that powers the whole city. It is not a verified place or a buildable design; we use it as a design brief for the form, not for its height or its claims (The Archetype).

Built in sections​

  • Phase 1 (built in Stage 5, Dec 2028 – Nov 2031): 90 m, three sections, on a foundation, basement and lift core sized for the final 240 m. A short temporary glass point sits on top.
  • Phase 2 (mid-2030s): 150 m, two more sections. The temporary point is lifted off and set back on top.
  • Phase 3: 240 m. The wind crown and the full glass point are added.
  • Precedent: towers built from stacked sections lifted into place, such as the Shukhov Tower (1922). Tall timber is proven to 85 m at Mjøstårnet; the spire uses timber inside (floors, halls, benches) and steel, glass and a concrete core for the height, because a 240 m tower needs harder materials.

The wind crown​

Between 150 and 204 m the crystal becomes a wind crown. The glass climbs on a slant, from about 150 m on one side to about 182 m on the other, and above it the white diagrid stays open, with slim members and a fine ring set in every other open diamond, so the wind passes straight through. Inside hang six helical vertical-axis turbines, about 9 m across and 18 m tall, three per level. The upper row is turned 60 degrees, so together the six fill every corner while a central mast runs up the middle to the point. Helical (Gorlov) blades are a kind of Darrieus turbine with similar or slightly lower efficiency but much smoother torque, which matters on a building (University of New Hampshire). Wind turbines have been built into towers before, at the Bahrain World Trade Centre and the Pearl River Tower, and the lessons are clear: vibration and noise must be designed out from the start.

  • Vibration. Rubber mounts under every turbine, speed control that brakes the rotors in storms, an open lattice that breaks up the gusts, the tuned water damper at 214 m, and no rooms on the turbine floors.
  • Power. Very roughly 0.5 to 1 GWh a year from the glass skin and the turbines together. This is a useful supplement and a visible lesson in clean energy, not the city's main supply, which comes from the solar fields and roofs.
  • Other uses. Antennas for radio and emergencies, a weather and fire watch, the lightning rod for the plaza, the gravity water tank and the basement batteries that help steady the town grid.
  • What it does not do. The spire makes no measurable "biofield" and no healing energy; any wellbeing effects of its halls and light are studied as open questions (Research).

Light at night​

Only shielded, warm light: a soft glow from inside the crystal and the required red aviation lights, with no uplighting or light shows, to protect wildlife and the stars (Light on the Land).

Terrestrial prototype (buildable kit-of-parts)​

Phase 1 (built in Stage 5, Dec 2028 – Nov 2031): 3 geodesic domes

  • Size: 30-45 m diameter (100-150 ft)
  • Height: 3-5 levels, 15-25 m tall
  • Capacity: 80-120 residents per dome
  • Total Phase 1: 240-360 residents
  • Structure: CLT (cross-laminated timber) + steel nodes, or tensegrity geodesic
  • Glazing: BIPV (building-integrated photovoltaics) + insulated panels

Phase 2 (mid-2030s, after Phase 1 proves out): 8 domes (five new at 44 m)

  • Size: 45-60 m diameter (150-200 ft)
  • Capacity: 120-200 residents per dome
  • Total in domes: 720-2,400 residents (the rest of the Village lives in ring housing)
  • Clustered: Outer ring arrangement, connected by covered walkways

Features - Terrestrial Model:

  • Resonance Maintenance: Acoustic design, ceremonial bell systems, geometric proportions
  • Community Integration: Shared commons, private units, maker spaces
  • Consciousness Connection: Regular gatherings, meditation spaces, knowledge-sharing circles
  • Partial Self-Sufficiency: Shared kitchens, gardens, rainwater systems
  • Ground Connection: Deep foundations, earth-coupling for thermal mass

Design Specifications:

  • Structure: Geodesic (icosahedron or similar), CLT frame, steel connectors
  • Envelope: R-30+ walls, R-50+ roof, triple-pane windows
  • Interior: 3-5 levels with central atrium, spiral stairs
  • Unit Mix: Studios (20%), 1-BR (40%), 2-BR (30%), 3-BR (10%)
  • Shared Spaces: Ground floor commons (200-400 m²), rooftop garden
  • Services: Composting toilets, greywater recycling, radiant heating/cooling

Living Experience:

  • Balance of privacy (private units) and community (shared meals 2-3x/week)
  • Connection through shared meals, councils and optional ceremonies
  • Natural alignment through biophilic design and acoustic environment
  • Knowledge sharing through workshops, skill exchanges, and study groups

Kit-of-Parts Approach:

  • Modular design allows phased construction
  • Each dome can be built independently
  • Shared infrastructure (energy, water, waste) connects domes
  • Future domes can increase in size as technology and funding allow

Energy Systems​

Energetic Architecture​

Light City operates on multiple energy levels:

Physical Energy (Conventional, TRL 8-9):

  • Solar PV: 2-5 MWp (Phase 1), 5-15 MWp (Phase 2)
  • Battery storage: 8-20 MWh (Phase 1), 40-80 MWh (Phase 2)

Relationship to Landscape​

Site Selection (Future)​

Siting Filters & Criteria:

Environmental:

  • Solar Insolation: ≥ 4.5 kWh/m²/day annual average (for PV viability)
  • Wind: Prevailing winds 5-15 mph for comfort; avoid extreme wind zones
  • Elevation: At least 350 ft (107 m) above sea level, clear of storm surge and coastal flooding
  • Topography: Gentle slopes (2-8%) for water terraces and passive solar
  • Soils: Class I-III agricultural soils for agroforestry (USDA classification)
  • Water: Secure, legally usable supply (springs, streams, wells or water rights); annual rainfall ≥ 30 inches preferred, or proven well yield and rights in drier regions
  • Geothermal / hydro (bonus): Hot-water resource for direct-use heat, or a year-round stream with head for micro-hydro

Infrastructure:

  • Grid Access: Within 5-10 miles of substation for interconnect (if hybrid system)
  • Road Access: Paved road within 2 miles; emergency vehicle access
  • Hospital: Within 30-60 minutes for medical emergencies
  • Airport: Within 2 hours for logistics and guest access

Legal & Social:

  • Zoning: Rural mixed-use, agricultural, or PD (Planned Development) overlay potential
  • Land ownership: A clear legal path for an international founding circle to own or lease the land
  • Water Rights: Secure water rights for domestic + agricultural use
  • Building Codes: Jurisdiction open to alternative building methods (CLT, geodesic)
  • Community: Supportive local community or remote enough to minimize conflict

Energetic (Visionary Layer):

  • Natural beauty and power
  • Connection to ley lines or energy vortices (if resonant)
  • Relatively undisturbed nature
  • Sacred recognition of land's spirit and history

Permitting Pathway​

Anticipated Approvals:

  • Land Use: Conditional Use Permit (CUP) or Planned Development (PD) rezoning
  • Environmental: Environmental Impact Report (EIR) or Assessment (EA)
  • Water: Well permits, water rights adjudication, greywater system approval
  • Septic/Wastewater: On-site treatment (constructed wetlands, composting) or district system
  • Building: Structural permits for tall tower (wind/seismic/ice loads), geodesic domes
  • Fire: Fire access roads, water storage, defensible space (if wildfire zone)
  • Electrical: Interconnection agreement with utility (if grid-tied)

Timeline: Permitting for Phase 1 typically 12-24 months depending on jurisdiction.

Architectural Inspirations​

Light City draws wisdom from existing sacred architecture on Earth. These structures show how geometry, contemplative design and spiritual intention can be expressed in stone and space:

Borobudur Temple - Indonesia​

Borobudur is one of the greatest Buddhist monuments, built in the 8th century. Its mandala structure, concentric platforms, and spiritual intentionality directly inform Light City design concepts.

Key Principles from Borobudur:

  • Concentric circular design (like Light City's rings)
  • Vertical ascent representing spiritual journey
  • Integration of sculpture, symbolism, and geometry
  • Multiple levels representing different states of consciousness
  • Sacred geometry embedded in proportions

Angkor Wat - Cambodia​

Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world, a masterpiece of Khmer architecture. Its design reflects cosmological principles and demonstrates how sacred geometry can organize vast spiritual spaces.

Key Principles from Angkor Wat:

  • Cosmological architecture reflecting the universe
  • Water integration (moats, reflection pools)
  • Sacred geometry in temple proportions
  • Hierarchical spatial organization
  • Symbolic journey from outer to inner sanctums
  • Stone craftsmanship at highest level

Design Application​

Both Borobudur and Angkor Wat demonstrate that:

  1. Large-scale spiritual spaces can be built with ancient techniques
  2. Sacred geometry works in creating consciousness-elevating environments
  3. Symbolic and practical can integrate seamlessly
  4. Community effort can realize a vision across generations

Light City builds on these principles with modern materials and engineering, and with respect for the traditions that created them.

Phase 1 Program & Areas (Pilot Hamlet)​

Central Plaza: 1-1.5 hectares (2.5-3.7 acres)

  • Gathering capacity: 500-1,000 people
  • Features: Central fountain, small outdoor fire circle for festivals, amphitheater seating
  • Materials: Stone, permeable paving, native plantings

Temple: 1,200-2,000 m² (13,000-21,500 sq ft)

  • Main hall: 600-1,000 m² with RT60 1.6-2.0 s
  • Meditation rooms: 200-400 m² with RT60 0.3-0.5 s
  • Support spaces: 400-600 m²

Studios/Maker Spaces: 1,500-3,000 m² (16,000-32,000 sq ft)

  • Art studios, woodshop, metal shop, ceramics, textiles
  • Shared equipment and tools
  • Natural light and ventilation

Communospheres: 3 domes @ 30-45 m diameter

  • Total units: 80-120 (mix of studio to 3-BR)
  • Shared commons: 600-1,200 m² total
  • Rooftop gardens: 1,000-2,000 m²

Gardens & Food: 4-6 hectares (10-15 acres)

  • Agroforestry: 1-2 ha
  • Annual gardens: 0.5-1 ha
  • Orchards: 1-2 ha
  • Wildlands/buffer: 1.5-2 ha

Operations Model (Steady-State)​

Resident Mix:

  • Founding members: 30-40% (equity holders)
  • Long-term residents: 40-50% (renters, co-op members)
  • Guests/researchers: 10-20% (short-term, revenue-generating)
  • Staff: 5-10% (on-site workers)

Revenue Streams:

  • Resident fees: $800-$1,500/month per person (housing + food + utilities)
  • Retreat programs: $150-$300/person/day (workshops, ceremonies)
  • Studio residencies: $1,000-$3,000/month (artists, researchers)
  • Events: Weddings, festivals, conferences
  • Agritourism: Farm tours, harvest experiences
  • Product sales: Crafts, food products, educational materials

Operating Expenses:

  • Staffing: $500K-$1.5M/year (10-20 FTE)
  • Utilities: $100K-$300K/year (supplemental grid, internet, phone)
  • Maintenance: $200K-$500K/year (buildings, equipment, grounds)
  • Food: $200K-$500K/year (supplemental purchases)
  • Insurance: $100K-$300K/year (property, liability, health)
  • Reserves: $200K-$500K/year (capital replacement, contingency)
  • TOTAL: $1.3M-$3.6M/year

Break-Even: Requires $400-$1,000/month net revenue per resident, achievable with mixed model.
