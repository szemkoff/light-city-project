Where it could grow

Five places, four countries, one first city.

No land has been bought. This desk study narrows the search to two US regions and three abroad, all above 350 ft, worth visiting in 2027. Each is strong in a different way.

What we look for

Land that can carry the whole idea.

The criteria come straight from the design brief: enough sun for the microgrid, water we have the right to use, codes that allow earth and timber, neighbors who welcome the project, and higher ground.

Energy

Sun first

At least 4.5 kWh/m²/day of sun for a 2–5 MWp solar microgrid. Hot water underground or a year-round stream is a bonus.

Water

Secure and legal

Rain, springs or wells, plus the rights to use them. In the dry Southwest, water rights decide everything.

Building

Earth and timber allowed

Codes that permit rammed earth, adobe, cob, compressed earth block and mass timber without years of variances.

Ground

Above 350 ft

Every site sits at least 350 ft (107 m) above sea level: clear of storm surge and coastal flooding, with cooler nights.

People

Welcome and reachable

A supportive local community, a legal path to own land, a hospital within an hour and an airport within about two hours.

Energy by region

Solar everywhere. No diesel anywhere.

Every option runs on solar and batteries first, with a regional grid tie. Backup comes from renewable gensets running on biogas from our own food waste or on bio-ethanol. Hydro and geothermal join only where the land offers them.

How we choose

From five regions to one piece of land.

  1. 2026

    Rank the criteria

    The founding circle decides what matters most: earth-building freedom, geothermal heat, water or community.

  2. Early 2027

    Shortlist two

    Talk with planners, water offices, land lawyers and local and Indigenous leaders before any land search.

  3. 2027

    Walk the land

    Visit 3–5 parcels per region and check sun, slope, soils, wells, streams and road access.

  4. 2027

    Test before buying

    Water-rights review, geotechnical survey and, where promising, a geothermal test well.

  5. End of Stage 2

    Choose the site

    The decision gate in the development stages. Only then does land change hands.
