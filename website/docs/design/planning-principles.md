# Planning Principles: Movement, Work and Water

How people get to work, where cars stop, and how water moves through the city decide whether a plan works day to day. This page brings together lessons from towns that have already solved these problems, adds the landscape rules of feng shui, and shows how the [3D reference model](pathname:///model/index.html?phase=3) now applies each one.

> **Our rule: live, work and meet within a short walk, with water at the heart and cars at the edge.**

## What other projects teach us

| Project | What it shows | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Auroville**, India | The Matrimandir stands in a Peace Area surrounded by a lake that may one day be a water source for the city ([Auroville](https://www.auroville.org/page/city-area)). The plan has four zones, a ring road, radial roads and a green belt, with only non-polluting vehicles inside the ring ([Auroville](https://www.auroville.org/page/town-planning)). | Water at the centre, fed by the city's water network; a green belt; clean vehicles only inside |
| **Auroville's Galaxy plan** | The city's zones turn round the centre like the arms of a galaxy instead of running in straight lines ([Auroville](https://www.auroville.org/page/galaxy-concept-of-the-city)) | Beyond the Phase 1 core, the plan turns gently so paths curve |
| **A Pattern Language**, "Scattered Work" | Spread workplaces through the city. Do not allow big areas of work with no homes nearby, or big areas of homes with no work nearby ([Pattern Language](https://patternlanguage.cc/Patterns/Scattered-Work-(9))) | A work hub in every neighbourhood and district |
| **A Pattern Language**, other patterns | Local transport areas with cars at the edge, access to water, accessible green, pools and streams ([summary](https://gauravsharma.com/design/christopher-alexander/pattern-language/)) | Public water edges; cars kept to the edge; water in reach of every home |
| **The 15-minute city** (Carlos Moreno) | Living, working, supplying, caring, learning and enjoying all within a short walk or ride; many centres, not one ([Moreno](https://hal.science/hal-04648637v1/file/07_id_c_moreno_bcns30_ang.pdf), [summary](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/719376136/The_15-_Minute_City_-_Summarised.pdf)) | Daily needs in each neighbourhood; the centre for what the whole city shares |
| **Ebenezer Howard's Social City** | A cluster of garden towns round a central city, linked by canals and railways, each with its own jobs and a permanent farm belt. Howard drew it as a diagram, to be fitted to the site ([CTBUH](https://global.ctbuh.org/resources/papers/download/390-the-vertical-garden-city-towards-a-new-urban-topology.pdf)) | Districts linked by water; food belts kept; the plan bends to the land |
| **Houten**, Netherlands | Cars use only a ring road, and each district is reached by car from one point on it. Bike paths run straight through the town, so cycling is often faster than driving. Because the town was planned with few jobs, many work trips still leave by car ([Houten](https://www.houten.nl/fileadmin/user_upload/Thema/Gemeentelijke_organisatie/Onderzoek_en_cijfers/FeitenCijfers2011/Europe_s_Vibrant_New_Low_Car_bon__Communities__Chapter_Houten.pdf), [PeopleForBikes](https://www.peopleforbikes.org/news/suburbia-reimagined-afternoon-houten-netherlands)) | Ring roads outside the city, one entry per district, and jobs inside, so the car ring is not the commute |
| **Vauban**, Freiburg | Cars park in two solar garages at the edge, and parking is sold separately from homes. Streets are car-free play streets, with shops and a tram stop within walking distance. Land is kept in reserve for a later garage and used as green space until then ([City of Freiburg](https://www.freiburg.de/pb/site/Freiburg/get/params_E-1730310368/1928877/Infotafeln_Vauban_en.pdf)) | Solar carports with shared EVs at the edge; land held back for later needs |
| **Barcelona superblocks** | Traffic kept to the edges of blocks about 400 m across. In Poblenou, traffic on inner streets fell 58% while traffic on the edges rose only 2.6% ([EBRD](https://ebrdgreencities.com/policy-tool/urban-planning-with-superblocks-barcelona-spain-2/)) | Cells of about 400–600 m with no through traffic |
| **Utrecht, Catharijnesingel** | A motorway turned back into a canal, making a 6 km boating ring round the old centre with a park and paths along it ([European Prize for Urban Public Space](https://www.publicspace.org/works/-/project/m357-catharijnesingel)) | Canal rings with parks along them, open to boats and walkers |
| **Room for the River**, Netherlands | Floodplains, bypass channels and water storage that lower flood risk and make better places at the same time ([Rijkswaterstaat](https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/en/projects/iconic-structures/room-for-the-river)) | Canals and creek banks that follow the land and store flood and drought water |
| **Candela P-12 electric ferry**, Stockholm | Commute cut from 55 to about 30 minutes, 94% less CO₂, 22.5% more passengers. It charges from a standard fast charger and leaves a small wake ([Candela](https://candela.com/newsroom/stockholms-electric-flying-ferry-successful-on-all-parameters/), [Candela P-12](https://candela.com/pro-series/p-12-shuttle/)) | Small, frequent electric water buses on the canal rings |

Masdar's lesson, from [Similar Movements](/docs/research/similar-movements), still applies: efficient buildings are proven, but car-free life only works if daily needs really are close by.

## Feng shui: reading the land

Feng shui is a traditional Chinese way of choosing and arranging a site. It is not a proven science, and we treat it as a design tradition. Many of its landscape rules agree with climate and ecology: shelter from cold winds, sun on the front, clean water close by and nothing aimed at the door. We use it where it agrees with those, and say so where it does not.

- **High ground behind, water in front.** Architects and texts describe the ideal site as backed by a mountain and belted with water in front. The *Water Dragon Classic* puts the water to the south, calm and smooth, never fast or straight ([Building and Environment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132304002197)).
- **The armchair.** The highest ground lies behind, ridges on both sides, and the front is open and sunny over lower land and water. Villages such as Lai Chi Wo in Hong Kong face the water and the sun and back onto the hill, with slow, winding streams ([HKU](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/260904/1/Content.pdf)).
- **Sit on the inside of the bend.** A site on the inside curve of a gentle river is favoured. Streets also carry qi, and should curve, because negative qi travels in straight lines ([Urban Feng Shui, University of Hawaiʻi](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4a03cf5d-6111-4f17-9999-a9b5fdba3337/content)).
- **Curves, not arrows.** Qi that follows winding paths is beneficial; straight roads aimed at a building are harmful ([Buildings, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/8/1593)).

In the northern hemisphere "behind" means north and "in front" means south. On a southern-hemisphere site the plan is mirrored, so the front still faces the sun.

### What the check found

| Feng shui test | The plan before | The plan now |
|---|---|---|
| High ground behind | Hills all round, none behind in particular | The highest hills behind to the north, a ridge on the east a little higher than the one on the west, an open south |
| Water in front, embracing | River to the north, bowing away from the city (a "reverse bow") | River to the south, curving round the whole town; the city sits on the inside of the bend |
| Water reaching the heart | The lake never reached the centre; the plaza pool stood alone | A winding heart canal brings boats from the lake to a harbour, and a stone rill carries the water on, between the domes, into the plaza pool |
| No straight arrows at the centre | 24 straight radial paths, all aimed at the spire | No straight lines: every path turns in a gentle spiral from the plaza, and the outer paths also wander a few metres |
| A quiet front, service behind | No defined road access | The service road comes from the north, behind the city; no road runs along the lake or river front |

The [sacred geometry](/docs/concepts/sacred-geometry) of the 12 paths is kept: they are still 12, evenly spaced, but they now run as the arms of a spiral rather than as spokes.

### Feng shui in the plan, point by point

Feng shui texts treat straight lines as the main thing to avoid. Straight roads and T-junctions aimed at a building are the classic "poison arrows", and qi is said to move well only along curving lines ([Feng Shui Practice](https://www.hpsst.com/uploads/6/2/9/3/62931075/chap_4_feng_shui_practice.pdf)). The second round of checks applied this to every path, road and building in the model:

- **No straight lines.** Each of the 12 paths leaves the plaza at an angle and turns in a slow logarithmic spiral, about 11° from a true radius, all the way out. Beyond the core a spiral is almost straight over one block, so the outer paths also wander a few metres to either side. The north road and the outer roads bend the same way. No path or road now runs straight for more than 80 m.
- **No path aimed at a dome.** The dome plots sit in the middle of each sector, between the radial paths, so the radials pass in the gaps between the domes and never run into a shell. Each dome's main doors face the plaza and the outer rings and open onto paved forecourts on the ring paths, which pass across the front rather than arriving head-on; smaller side doors open onto the radials as they go by. Earlier versions put the domes on the radials, first with planted beds and trees across the paths in front of the doors (a too-literal reading of the rule that blocked the way in) and then with the path running through the dome. Both were replaced. The two ring paths near the dome ring bend round each dome instead of cutting through it. Paths that would end at a blank wall (work hubs, district squares, workshop rows) still get a small planted bed beside the path, never across it.
- **The bright hall (ming tang).** The open space in front of a site, where qi gathers, should be open, level and calm ([JCBAR](https://ojs.bonviewpress.com/index.php/JCBAR/article/download/4706/1506)). The plaza, the dome forecourts and the lake front stay open: no buildings or roads on them.
- **Arrive, don't block.** A path that ends at a door is fixed by arriving well, not by planting it shut. Every path is continuous: the radials join the plaza to the outer rings without a break, and the ring paths curve round the shells past each forecourt, so a reserved nursery plot never cuts a route. Planted beds are used only where a path would otherwise end at a blank wall. This matches the tradition (qi should arrive and gather in the ming tang, not be cut off) and everyday needs: people, bikes, deliveries and emergency crews need a clear way in and out of every building.
- **The water mouth (shuikou).** Where water leaves a village, Huizhou villages "lock" it with a grove, a bridge and a pavilion, so that the water and its good fortune are not lost easily ([Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/25875955.pdf)). Shuikou woodland also measurably calms the wind around villages ([Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-45973-5)). In the model, where the creek meets the river, there is now a grove, a pavilion and a bridge. Next to them, the treatment wetland cleans the city's water before it leaves (see [Infrastructure](/docs/design/infrastructure)).
- **Feng shui woods behind.** Villages in southern China keep a crescent of forest on the slope behind them. It shelters them from wind, keeps moisture and soil on the hill and cools the summer ([HKU Forest Village](https://ccsg.hku.hk/forestvillage/index.php?route=information/page&page_id=28), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/travel/article/20201215-the-tiny-forests-designed-by-feng-shui)). The model now has a dense crescent of woodland north of the final city, from about 1,070 m to 1,400 m. It is planted in Phase 1, so it is mature by Phase 3, and the utility campus sits inside it.
- **A curving harbour.** The heart canal used to end in a round basin, which read as a blunt end. The harbour is now a crescent lying along the ring. The canal enters at one horn, the rill leaves from the other, and trees frame the landward side.

### Collisions and the layout audit

A layout audit now runs on the model (`node website/scripts/audit-model.cjs`). For each phase it checks for buildings on paths or in water, buildings overlapping each other, path ends aimed at doors, straight runs over 80 m and trees on paths. The first run found real clashes:

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Phase 2 carports on the 480 m ring road and on a Phase 3 terrace path | Hubs moved to 506 m with two rows of roofs; the ring path runs into the paved hub square and out the other side |
| Ring path through each Phase 2 work hub's market hall | The ring path ends at the edge of the hub square, with a planted bed |
| Ring-5 solar arrays on the spiral paths and the rill | Two arrays per sector, set clear of the paths |
| Workshops overlapping each other at the work hubs | Set further apart round the square |
| Terrace buildings at 510 m touching at the mid-crescent passage | Wider passage and a wider gap at each path |
| Greenhouses end to end in the food belts | Spaced along the rows |
| 24 to 29 trees standing on paths | Trees are now cleared off every path and road; a tree under a later phase's path is felled when that path is built |

After the fixes the audit reports, in every phase: 0 buildings on paths or in water, 0 overlaps, 0 straight runs over 80 m and 0 trees on paths. Every path end that meets a building is screened by planting.

## Movement

**Each way of moving has its place:**

1. **Walking.** Every home is within about 4 minutes' walk of a work hub and market (about 300 m). In Phase 3, the plaza is about 12 minutes on foot from the outer edge.
2. **Cycling and e-bikes.** The curving radial paths double as bike routes. From the edge to the plaza takes about 4 minutes.
3. **Boats.** Electric water buses run on the canal rings, the heart canal and the lake. The heart canal brings boats to a harbour 238 m from the spire.
4. **Shuttles.** Small electric shuttles run between the edge hubs and the work hubs. In Phase 3 they use the Phase 2 ring road, which becomes the shuttle and service loop.
5. **Cars, at the edge.** Shared EVs sit under solar carports at mobility hubs on the ring roads. Each district is reached by car from one point, and parking is sold separately from homes.

**Mobility hubs by phase:**

| Phase | Road | Hubs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A service road from the north, through the valley in the hills | One hub at 252 m with about 30 shared cars, e-bikes and a shuttle stop |
| 2 | A ring road at 480 m round the back and sides, starting and ending in the two southern hubs, so the lake and river front stay quiet | Three hubs at 45°, 165° and 300° |
| 3 | An outer ring road at 1,035 m outside the food belt; the 480 m ring becomes the shuttle loop | Six hubs, one per district |

## Work and daily flows

A town with all its jobs in the centre fills its roads inward every morning and outward every evening. Light City spreads work across the city instead:

- **Phase 1.** The studios on ring 3 and the learning pavilions are the workplaces, all within 3 minutes' walk.
- **Phase 2.** Three neighbourhood work hubs on the 285 m ring, each with a timber market hall, three workshops with solar roofs and a small square. They replace two courtyard clusters each.
- **Phase 3.** Each of the six district centres adds a market hall and a row of five workshops behind its 80 m dome, next to the district temple.

**What this does to the morning and evening.** Most people walk a few minutes to work in their own neighbourhood, so trips run in every direction rather than all toward the centre. The centre is for what the whole city shares (the temple, learning, festivals and the market by the spire), and those visits spread across the day. The few trips out of town leave by shuttle from the edge hubs.

## Water

- **Phase 1.** A lake south of the core, fed by rain and the land, spills down a winding creek to the river. A heart canal winds from the lake to a harbour at the edge of the core. From there a narrow stone rill, about 2–3 m wide, carries the water between the domes into the ring pool around the spire. Whether it flows by gravity or with solar pumping depends on the site's slope.
- **Phase 2.** A canal ring at 377 m runs through the lake.
- **Phase 3.** An outer canal ring at 746 m and three S-curved canals link the rings, the lake and the river. Banks are public paths and parks, and are shaped to store flood and drought water.

The real courses will follow the site's landform and drainage. The model shows the intent, not a survey.

## Communospheres: what is inside

A communosphere is a home for about a hundred people, not a greenhouse ([Communospheres](/docs/concepts/communospheres)). The model now shows that:

- **The shell.** A timber gridshell with clear glazing low down for the homes, solar glass on the sunny side and green-roof panels on the shaded side. An open oculus at the crown lets warm air out.
- **Terraced homes.** Three levels in a 40 m dome, four in a 44 m dome and five in an 80 m district dome. Each level steps back toward the shell, so the homes look onto the atrium over planted terraces.
- **Shared kitchen and commons** on the ground floor round the atrium.
- **A small garden atrium** under the oculus: a lawn, a rain pool, three trees and some shrubs. The trees give shade, cool the air as they breathe out moisture, soften sound and give the shared space a living centre.

## How we will test it on a real site

- Map the slope and drainage first, then fit the water network to them; the heart rill only flows by gravity if the core sits higher than the lake.
- Check winter winds and summer breezes, and place the hills, woodland and canals to shelter and cool.
- Model trips for a typical weekday: share of trips on foot, by bike, by boat and by car, and the busiest flows on each path.
- Ask residents, as Barcelona did, before closing any street to traffic.
