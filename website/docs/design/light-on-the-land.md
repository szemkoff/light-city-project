# Light on the Land: Minimal Impact on Nature

A new settlement always changes the land it sits on. Zero impact is not possible: we will clear some ground, use some materials and bring people who eat, travel and buy things. What *is* possible today is a city that takes much less than a normal town, gives some of it back, and proves it with published measurements. This page sets out what proven technology can do, what real projects have already achieved, where the hard limits are, and the commitments Light City will hold itself to.

> **Our rule: the land stays mostly wild.** Build small and compact, power and water ourselves, protect the night, and leave the site with more life on it than we found.

---

## Is it really possible? What has already been done

These are not concepts. Each one has been built, measured and independently reported.

| Project | What it achieved | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Findhorn Ecovillage**, Scotland | Residents' ecological footprint of 2.71 global hectares per person, about half the UK average of 5.4, the lowest then measured for any community in the industrialised world | [Findhorn footprint report, 2006](https://base.socioeco.org/docs/ff_20footprint.pdf) |
| **Sieben Linden Ecovillage**, Germany | 5.0 t CO₂ per person per year, 52% below the German average of 10.5 t; housing about 0.3 t versus 2.8 t | [Sieben Linden, 2026](https://siebenlinden.org/de/oekologischer-fussabdruck/) |
| **Sieben Linden straw-bale homes** | Heating use cut by 90% and per-person CO₂ by 72% versus the German average | [World Habitat](https://world-habitat.org/awards/winners/straw-bale-housing-in-the-sieben-linden-ecovillage/) |
| **Kendeda Building**, Georgia Tech, USA | Certified Living Building: net-positive energy and water over a full year, rainwater for drinking, greywater treated in a constructed wetland, composting toilets | [Georgia Tech](https://livingbuilding.gatech.edu/) |
| **Agrivoltaic farms**, USA | Sheep graze under solar panels and gain weight like open-pasture sheep; pollinator plantings at solar sites drew about four times as many pollinator visits | [NLR](https://www.nlr.gov/solar/market-research-analysis/agrivoltaics), [USDA Climate Hubs](https://www.climatehubs.usda.gov/hubs/northwest/topic/agrivoltaics-pairing-solar-power-and-agriculture-northwest) |
| **England's biodiversity law** | Since February 2024, most new developments must leave at least 10% more habitat value than before, measured with a statutory metric and maintained for 30 years | [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/understanding-biodiversity-net-gain) |

**What this tells us.** A community of a few hundred people, living in well-insulated homes, eating mostly local and plant-forward food and sharing cars and tools, can run at roughly half a normal footprint with technology that exists now. Buildings can produce more energy and water than they use. New development can leave more nature behind, not less.

**The honest part.** Even Sieben Linden says its 5 tonnes per person is still about five times the planet's fair share ([Sieben Linden](https://siebenlinden.org/de/oekologischer-fussabdruck/)). Findhorn's headline figure left out guests' flights to its courses ([Findhorn footprint report](https://base.socioeco.org/docs/ff_20footprint.pdf)). Lifestyle and travel matter as much as buildings, and we will count both.

## Where a Light City has real impact, and what to do about it

| Impact | Why it matters | What we do | Readiness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land taken** | Every building and road replaces habitat | Compact core of 20–30 acres for Phase 1 on a 100–500 acre site; build on already farmed or degraded ground, never old forest or wetland; keep at least half the site as permanent habitat | **Proven** (planning choice) |
| **Biodiversity** | Fragmented, sprayed land loses species | Baseline ecological survey before design; wildlife corridors kept open between habitat patches; native planting; no pesticides; aim for at least 20% biodiversity net gain using a recognised metric | **Proven** method, needs ecologists |
| **Energy** | Fossil fuel is the largest climate impact | All-electric daily life with no fossil fuels; renewable-fuel backup only during long outages; rooftop solar plus agrivoltaic fields with grazing and pollinator meadows; batteries; aim for 105% or more of annual use, the Living Building threshold | **Proven** |
| **Water** | Arid sites can drain aquifers | Rainwater harvesting, low-flow fixtures, greywater reuse through constructed wetlands, composting or vacuum toilets; no net drawdown of groundwater | **Proven**, but permits for drinking rainwater vary by country |
| **Materials and carbon** | Concrete, steel and glass carry heavy embodied carbon | Local stone, earth, timber and bamboo; minimal concrete; measure embodied carbon for every building and publish it (see [Beyond the Box](/docs/design/organic-forms-and-materials)) | **Proven** to Emerging |
| **Night light** | Artificial light at night disrupts birds, bats, insects and sleep | Outdoor lights shielded and pointed down, warm color (3000 K or warmer), dimmed or off when not needed, no uplighting of the spire or domes; work toward International Dark Sky Community status | **Proven** ([DarkSky](https://darksky.org/what-we-do/international-dark-sky-places/apply/)) |
| **Glass and birds** | Glass kills up to a billion birds a year in North America, mostly on low buildings | Bird-safe fritted or UV-patterned glass on all domes and large windows, following the 2 by 4 inch spacing rule; overhangs and screens | **Proven** ([USFWS](https://www.fws.gov/story/federal-agencies-treating-glass-reduce-bird-collisions)) |
| **Cars and roads** | Roads split habitat and bring noise and runoff | Car-free core; shared electric vehicles parked at the edge; walking and cycling paths of permeable surfaces | **Proven** |
| **Waste** | Landfill and sewage leave the site | Composting, repair and sharing culture, construction waste sorted and reused; sewage treated on site as a resource | **Proven** |
| **Construction itself** | Grading, compaction and erosion damage soil for decades | Save and replace topsoil; minimal grading; fence off trees and streams during works; restore disturbed areas in the next planting season | **Proven** |

## What this means for the design and the images

Some of our earlier imagery showed things that would *increase* impact, and we are correcting them:

- **Glass domes.** Large glass areas risk bird strikes, overheating and high energy use. Domes need bird-safe patterned glass, shading, operable vents and a limited glass-to-solid ratio. Solid green-roofed sections are as important as glazed ones.
- **Night lighting.** A city that glows at night harms wildlife and hides the stars. The "light" in Light City is inside: daylight, warm rooms and people. Outdoors after dark it should look like a quiet village, not a stage set.
- **Fire.** Open fire indoors is a health and fire-code problem and burns fuel. Indoor gathering rooms use daylight, warm lamps and water features. Fire stays outdoors, small and occasional.
- **Landscape.** Most of what you see around the buildings should be wild meadow, forest, farms and water, not lawns and paving.

## Ten commitments we will measure and publish

1. **Build small.** Developed area stays at or under 15% of the site; at least 50% is placed under a permanent conservation agreement.
2. **More life after than before.** At least 20% biodiversity net gain against a pre-construction baseline, monitored for 30 years.
3. **Net-positive energy.** 105% or more of annual energy from on-site renewables, with no fossil fuel; renewable-fuel backup runs only during long outages.
4. **Live within the rain.** No net groundwater drawdown; water reused at least once before it returns to the land.
5. **Count the carbon in buildings.** Embodied carbon measured and published for every building, with a falling target each phase.
6. **Protect the night.** DarkSky-compliant outdoor lighting from day one.
7. **Safe glass.** Bird-safe glass on every dome and large window.
8. **A car-free core.** Shared electric vehicles at the edge only.
9. **Half the footprint.** Residents' average carbon footprint at or below half the national average of the host country by the third year of occupancy, including travel.
10. **Show the numbers.** An annual, independently reviewed impact report, published whether the results are good or bad.

These targets will be tested during Stage 2 (Design) against the chosen site's climate, ecology and laws, and tightened where possible.
