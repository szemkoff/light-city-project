# Economy and Migration: From Fees to Light Hours

**Status**: Draft design for Stage 2 (Design, 2027)  
**Last reviewed**: November 2026

:::caution Draft, not yet a legal or tax structure
This page describes the intended direction. The legal form, tax treatment and exact thresholds will be set with legal and tax counsel during Stage 2, before any Light hours are issued. Nothing here is an offer of securities or a promise of a specific fee.
:::

## In one paragraph

Light City cannot get rid of money at its edge: land, loan payments, investors, taxes, insurance and anything bought from outside are paid in dollars. It can make money disappear **inside** the community. The design has two layers. A small **dollar layer at the edge**, run by the community entity on everyone's behalf, and an **inside economy** that moves in four phases from monthly fees, to **Light hours** (a time credit), to basics provided for doing your share. A **dial** sets how much of each home's monthly fee can be paid in Light hours. The dial only moves up after a measured gate is passed and residents vote for it. The last phase, an economy with no money inside, is an **option residents can vote for**, reviewed every year.

## Two layers

| Layer | What it covers | Paid in |
|---|---|---|
| **The edge** | Loan payments and investor returns, taxes, insurance, materials and goods bought from outside, outside services, reserves | Dollars, earned by community businesses and, in early phases, by home fees |
| **The inside** | Meals, maintenance, gardens, childcare, elder care, learning, workshops, events, wellness, energy and water use | Dollars at first, then Light hours, then provided for doing your share |

**The edge bill** is the total of the edge costs each year. Money stops being needed inside the community once community businesses (retreats, licensing, products, farm sales) earn enough dollars to pay the edge bill. Every gate below is built around that one test.

## Light hours

Light hours are the community's time credit.

- **One hour of work earns one Light hour**, whatever the work. An hour of cooking, childcare or elder care counts the same as an hour of building or accounting.
- **Every account starts at zero.** There is no interest on positive or negative balances.
- **Balances have limits.** Draft range: down to −40 and up to +200 Light hours per adult. Hours above your weekly share can be banked for time off, retreats or travel.
- **Light hours cannot be bought with dollars or turned into dollars.** They have no cash value, including when a resident leaves. New residents start at zero.
- **Work must be part of the shared plan.** Creditable work is set by the area circles and published, so everyone can see what counts.
- **Care counts.** Sick time, parental time and elder care earn Light hours under published rules. Children and residents who cannot work are covered by the community, not by their own balance.
- **The ledger is open.** Every resident can see totals, limits and how many accounts are near a limit. Individual entries are visible only to the account holder and the ledger stewards.

### What Light hours pay for, by phase

| Phase | Light hours can pay for |
|---|---|
| 0 · Pilot | Extra meals, workshops, tool and studio time, childcare swaps |
| 1 · Two currencies | The per-person fees for extra adults and children, plus everything in Phase 0. Contribution-based residency tiers are counted in Light hours |
| 2 · Basics for your share | Meeting your weekly share earns housing, meals, energy and learning. Light hours above your share pay for extras |
| 3 · Provisioning (option) | Light hours track contribution and time off only. Basics and services are provided without payment |

## The four-phase dial

The **dial** is the share of each home's monthly fee that can be paid in Light hours instead of dollars. Paying in dollars is always allowed in Phases 0–2, so nobody is forced to switch before they are ready.

| Phase | When | How it works | Dial (share payable in Light hours) | Gate to the next phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Pilot** | 2027–2028 residency pilot | Fees in dollars as today. A Light hours pilot runs for meals, workshops and care. | 0% of the home fee | The pilot ledger stays healthy for 12 months and most participants call the system fair |
| **1 · Two currencies** | Move-in, 2031 | Home fee in dollars. Per-person fees and contribution tiers in Light hours. | 20–30% | Community businesses cover the full loan payments for 12 months |
| **2 · Basics for your share** | About 3–5 years after opening | Meeting your weekly share earns housing, food and energy. Dollars pay only a shrinking household share of the edge bill. | 50–90%, stepped | At least half of food and most energy produced on site, and businesses cover at least 75% of the edge bill |
| **3 · Provisioning (option)** | Only if residents vote for it | No money inside the community. Dollars exist only at the edge, earned and spent by the community entity. | 100% | Yearly review gate (below). If it is missed, the community returns to Phase 2 |

### How the dial moves

1. **Up one step at a time.** The dial can only move up after the gate for that step is met and published.
2. **Residents decide.** Each move up needs an 80% consent vote of residents, the same threshold [governance](/docs/project/governance) uses for major decisions.
3. **Down when the numbers say so.** If the edge bill is not covered for two quarters in a row, the dial steps back one step until the gate is met again. This needs no vote, so the community can never vote itself into missing a loan payment.
4. **Never below the edge bill.** The dollar share of fees, plus business income, must always cover the edge bill and reserves. This is the line investors can rely on.

## Phase 3: an option, reviewed every year

Phase 3 is **not a promise**. It is an option the residents can choose once the community can carry it.

- **Opting in.** Residents can vote to enter Phase 3 once the Phase 2 gate has been met for at least 12 months. The vote needs 80% consent.
- **The yearly review gate.** Each year the community council publishes a review and residents vote again to stay in Phase 3. The review covers:
  - **Edge bill:** community businesses cover 100% of the edge bill and keep at least 12 months of reserves.
  - **Self-production:** the share of food, energy and water produced on site, compared with the year before.
  - **Ledger health:** how many residents are at or near their Light hours limits, and whether the weekly share is being met.
  - **Fairness:** a resident survey on workload, burnout and whether the system feels fair.
- **If the gate is missed or the vote fails,** the community returns to Phase 2 at the start of the next quarter. It can vote to re-enter Phase 3 once the gate is met again.

## The weekly share

The **weekly share** is the number of hours each adult contributes to keep the inside economy running. Residents set it each year from the published work plan, the same way Twin Oaks sets its weekly quota ([Twin Oaks labor policy](https://www.twinoaks.org/policies/labor-policy)). Draft starting point: 20–25 hours per adult per week, lower for parents of young children, elders and anyone on agreed leave. The existing [contribution-based residency](/docs/project/contribution-based-residency) tiers (25–30, 15–20 and 8–12 hours a week) become the first version of the weekly share.

## What this means for the financial model

The operating budget of about $2.5M a year mixes two kinds of cost: work residents could do (cooking, gardening, cleaning, maintenance, childcare, events) and true edge costs. Each hour residents take on is a dollar the community no longer needs to raise. The Stage 2 model will split the operating budget into these two parts and show the edge bill on its own, so every gate can be measured. See the [Household Model](/docs/project/development-stages#household-model-november-2026-draft) for the current dollar figures.

## For investors

- Investors are repaid in dollars, from community business income and the dollar share of fees.
- The dial can never move below the edge bill, and it steps back automatically if the edge bill is not covered.
- Light hours are not convertible and are not offered to investors.

## Tax and legal

- In the US, the IRS treats barter income and trade dollars as taxable at fair market value, and barter exchanges must file Form 1099-B. It makes an exception for informal, non-commercial exchanges such as a babysitting co-op ([IRS Topic 420](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc420)). How Light hours are treated will depend on the legal structure chosen with counsel.
- Taxes are paid in national currency. Sardex advised members to keep credit trade to about 30% of their business for this reason ([DW](https://www.dw.com/en/italys-b2b-cashless-sardex-currency-set-to-take-on-the-world/a-45300395)). This is why the dial moves in steps and the edge stays in dollars.
- The rules will also follow the law of the country where the site is chosen.

## What others teach us

| Community or system | What it does | Lesson for Light City |
|---|---|---|
| **Twin Oaks** (Virginia, US) | Members work about 38.5 hours a week and receive housing, food, clothing and health care, with no individual wages ([Twin Oaks FAQ](https://www.twinoaks.org/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-85)). One hour equals one labor credit, and credits have no cash value ([labor policy](https://www.twinoaks.org/policies/labor-policy)). Dollars come from community businesses such as tofu and hammocks ([Berghahn Journals](https://www.berghahnjournals.com/view/journals/social-analysis/66/3/sa660302.xml)). | Equal hours work for decades when the quota, banked hours and care rules are clear. Businesses pay the edge. |
| **Sardex** (Sardinia, Italy) | Mutual credit among about 3,000 businesses. Accounts start at zero, carry no interest and have capped credit lines ([LSE paper](http://eprints.lse.ac.uk/67622/1/Dini_Self-funded%20social.pdf)). | Limits and a zero starting point stop hoarding and runaway debt. |
| **WIR** (Switzerland) | A cooperative business currency since 1934, used alongside the Swiss franc and not convertible into it ([Levy Institute](https://www.levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/pn_16_1.pdf)). | Two currencies can run side by side for a very long time. Taxes stay in national currency. |
| **Auroville** (India) | Aims for no money changing hands among residents and provides health care, education and classes in return for work and monthly contributions ([Auroville A to Z](https://www.auroville.org/page/auroville-a-to--z)). It still relies on outside support and grows about 15% of its own food. | Don't promise self-sufficiency on a date. Measure it, and gate each step on it. |

## Open questions

- Which legal structure lets Light hours run without creating taxable barter income for every hour, and in which country?
- What weekly share is sustainable without burnout, and how should skilled outside-facing work (such as retreat facilitation) be counted?
- What balance limits work best for households with children?
- Which ledger tool: a simple open-source mutual-credit ledger first, with any blockchain or token option studied separately and never on the critical path.
