# The Living Archive

*The city's open memory: a spiral gallery inside the spire, and a record anyone can read, add to and copy*

The visionary source describes the Arcturian city of Delphin keeping a "living archive" of the history of the star seeds, one you can interact with and that "will provide you with the knowledge". The same account describes the Hall of Amenti, where all Lemurian knowledge resided, and the Lemurians as "illuminators" who shared what they knew freely, until elites who hoarded technology tried to erase them and their history ([recording transcript](https://github.com/szemkoff/light-city-project/blob/main/research/spiritual/arcturian-crystal-city-transcript.md)). We treat that as inspiration, not fact ([The Archetype](/docs/concepts/the-archetype)). What we take from it is a building and a rule.

- **The building:** a place where the city's story is kept and can be explored, not just stored.
- **The rule:** knowledge in Light City is open. No person or group gains standing by controlling a system, a plan or a record, and nothing in the archive can be quietly removed.

## What it holds

| Collection | What is in it | How it grows |
|---|---|---|
| **The land before the city** | Surveys of soil, water, plants and animals, old maps, and the history of the people who lived there first, told with them | Baseline survey in Stage 1, re-surveyed every five years |
| **How the city was made** | Every plan, model version, decision and budget, with the reasons and the dissent. The 3D model and this library are part of it | Each council decision and each built change is filed when it is made |
| **Voices** | Recorded oral histories from residents, builders and visitors, in their own words, with consent | A recording booth in the archive and in every communosphere ([StoryCorps](https://storycorps.org/) is a model) |
| **The living systems** | Live and past readings of water, air, soil, energy and biodiversity, the city's "vital signs" | Sensors and the [digital twin](/docs/glossary); published openly |
| **Seeds and plants** | A seed library of the crops, trees and wild plants grown in the city, kept cool and dry, and lent out each spring | Residents save and return seed ([Seed Savers Exchange](https://www.seedsavers.org/) is a model); a duplicate set goes to a national seed bank, in the spirit of the [Svalbard Global Seed Vault](https://www.seedvault.no/) |
| **Skills** | How to run, repair and rebuild each system in the city, written by the people who do it | Each team keeps its own handbook up to date |
| **The visionary sources** | The channelled material the idea grew from, in full, clearly labelled as inspiration | Added as it is shared, graded in the [bibliography](/docs/research/bibliography) |

The "star seed" history in the source becomes, on Earth, a literal seed library: the seeds the city lives on, kept and shared by everyone.

## Where it is

**In the spire (Phase 2).** The archive fills the spire from 94 m to 118 m. The library is on six level floors, 4 m apart, stacked round the lift core, each about 950 m². Its shelves stand in arcs round the core and its reading areas face inward, away from the bright glass, so books and seeds stay out of direct sun. A ramp runs right along the windows, outside the library floors, and rises 4 m in each full turn: about 125 m of walking per floor at a slope of 3.1 to 3.2%, never more than 3.5%, close to the 2 degrees of the [Seattle Central Library's Books Spiral](https://special.seattletimes.com/o/news/local/library/architecture/bookspiral.html). It passes every floor and ends at the learning level at 118 m, where the archive's data feeds the weather station and the climate lab. The lifts stop at every floor, so nobody has to walk the whole climb. [See it in the 3D model](pathname:///model/index.html?phase=2&view=spire).

- **Feng shui:** the ramp has no straight run and no corner, like every path in the city. The archive sits high in the tower, behind the open ming tang of the Spire Park, a quiet place for something meant to last.
- **Accessible by design:** below a 1:20 (5%) slope a walkway is not a ramp under the [ADA Standards](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch04/), which allow up to 1:12 with a landing for every 0.76 m of rise. At about 3.2%, wheelchairs, prams and book trolleys move freely, and each library floor works as a level resting place.
- **Phase 1:** until the Phase 2 sections are built, the archive starts as exhibitions in the base hall and a reading room in the learning pavilion.
- **The vault:** originals, the seed library and backup drives are kept in the spire basement, which is cool, dry, stable and has its own power. A second copy is kept off site.
- **In every communosphere:** a small archive corner with a recording booth and a screen, linked to the main archive. This is the everyday version of the source's idea that what one sphere learns, all spheres know.

## Structure, materials and fire safety

Paper is a heavy fire load, and the archive is 100 m up, so it is built to burn as little as possible and to stop a fire where it starts.

- **Structure:** steel and concrete, as for the rest of the spire. US code allows a mass-timber structure only up to 270 ft (82 m), and only fully encased ([WoodWorks](https://www.woodworks.org/wp-content/uploads/wood_solution_paper-tall-wood.pdf)); the archive starts above that.
- **Floors and ramp:** stone or terrazzo, which do not burn, last for generations and are easy to clean.
- **Walls:** clay plaster, which is non-combustible, has very low emissions and buffers indoor humidity in the healthy 40–60% range ([EPFL HOBEL](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/hobel/the-effects-of-bio-based-construction-materials-on-indoor-air-quality/)). That steady humidity also protects paper and seeds.
- **Ceilings:** warm oak plank ceilings, made of real wood veneer on a non-combustible fibre-gypsum core, rated A2-s1,d0, the highest class wood products can reach ([Gustafs Linear Plank-P](https://gustafs.com/product/linear-plank-p-ceiling/), [fire safety](https://gustafs.com/solutions/fire-safety-wood-interiors/)). They look like timber planking but need no chemical fire retardants. Acoustic versions of the same planks soften sound on the open floors.
- **Warm wood where people touch it:** real wood veneer on a non-combustible fibre-gypsum core, rated A2-s1,d0 and ASTM Class A ([Gustafs](https://gustafs.com/product/wood-panel/)), for shelf ends, reading-room walls and the core. Ordinary fire-retardant plywood is not enough: once a decorative veneer goes on, some panels drop back to Class C ([Columbia Forest Products](https://www.columbiaforestproducts.com/plywood/firebrand)). Solid wood is kept to handrails, tables and chairs.
- **Shelving:** powder-coated steel in a warm bronze tone, with the veneer end panels.
- **Fire protection:** the archive is its own fire compartment, with fire-rated floors and doors at the core and smoke extraction through the spire's double glass skin, which already works as a solar chimney. The library floors use water mist, which puts out a fire with far less water than sprinklers and is the least damaging to most collections ([Society of American Archivists](https://www2.archivists.org/sites/all/files/Section%204%20-%20Fire.pdf), [NEDCC](https://www.nedcc.org/free-resources/preservation-leaflets/3.-emergency-management/3.2-introduction-to-fire-detection-and-automatic-sprinklers-for-cultural-heritage)). The basement vault uses a clean-agent gas system that leaves no residue ([National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/museums/upload/MHI_Ch9_FireProtection.pdf)), designed to [NFPA 909](https://www.nfpa.org/product/nfpa-909-code/p0909code/nfpa-909-code-for-the-protection-of-cultural-resource-properties-museums-libraries-and-places-of-worship-2021/90921) for libraries and museums.
- **Weight:** book stacks are among the heaviest floor loads, so the upper floors hold the working collection, displays and digital access, and the heavy originals stay in the basement vault.

## How it works

- **Open by default.** Everything is published online and free to copy under open licences ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/)), the way the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) keeps the web. The exceptions are private: personal data, and stories residents choose to keep closed for a time.
- **Interactive, not just stored.** Visitors can ask questions at tables in the gallery and get answers drawn from the collections, with the source shown each time. The tools are open, and a person, not the software, is always the keeper of the record.
- **Nothing quietly erased.** Records are versioned. A correction adds a new version and keeps the old one. Removing something needs a public council decision, and the reason is kept.
- **Run by keepers.** A small team of resident archivists, on a rotation open to anyone who trains, looks after the collections and helps visitors. Keeping the archive is a shared role, not a profession that owns it.
- **Labelled honestly.** Every item carries its grade: measured data, documented record, personal account or visionary source. Readers always know which is which.

## Why it matters

A city built to last generations needs to remember why it was built the way it was, or the next generation will repeat old mistakes or undo good decisions without knowing why. The archive also shows that the project keeps its promises: anyone can check the plans, the money and the results. And it holds the source's best idea in a form that needs no belief: knowledge shared freely makes everyone stronger, and knowledge hoarded makes a few people powerful.
