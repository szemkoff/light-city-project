Homes & daily life

Private homes, shared abundance.

Five home types, from a tiny home for one to a three-bedroom family home. One all-inclusive monthly fee. Every home sits within walking distance of shared gardens, kitchens, studios and the plaza.

Phase 1 figures for the 200-resident Pilot Hamlet, planned as about 110 homes. Sizes, mix and prices are planning ranges and will be finalized during Stage 2 (Design, 2027) once a site is chosen. First residents are targeted for 2031.

Home types

Choose the scale of private life you need.

Tiny home · 200–300 sf

Tiny home

$500–700 / month

A self-contained curved-timber home with a sleeping niche, a compact kitchen and bath, big windows and a deck. Tiny homes are grouped in fours around a shared courtyard with planted beds and stone seating, so neighbors share outdoor space naturally.

  • Best for: singles, artists, newcomers and contribution-based residents
  • Where: garden clusters on the outer ring, a short walk from a communosphere
  • Built with: prefabricated CLT panels, a planted roof and a heat pump, or as an earth tiny home

Studio · 400–500 sf

Studio

$700–950 / month

A bright, efficient apartment terraced into the inner levels of a communosphere. A bed alcove, kitchenette and private bath, with windows over the dome's indoor garden. Climate is gentle all year because the dome shell buffers wind and cold.

  • Best for: individuals who want privacy with community a few steps away
  • Where: inside the domes, on levels 1–4 around the central atrium
  • Includes: private bath, kitchenette and access to the dome's shared kitchen

1-bedroom · 600–800 sf

One-bedroom

$950–1,300 / month

A full apartment with a separate bedroom, a full kitchen, room to work from home and a planted balcony over the dome atrium. It suits couples or anyone who cooks, hosts or needs a quiet workspace.

  • Best for: couples, remote workers and long-term residents
  • Where: inside the domes, on upper terraces with the best daylight
  • Includes: full kitchen, private balcony and a workspace nook

2-bedroom · 900–1,200 sf

Two-bedroom family home

$1,300–1,700 / month

A two-story timber home with its own front garden on a car-free lane. Children can roam safely between homes, gardens and the learning spaces. Rooftop solar, a green roof and a heat pump keep running costs, which are already included, low.

  • Best for: a family of three or four, two adults sharing, or a couple with a parent
  • Where: ring housing between the domes and the food-growing edge
  • Includes: private garden, storage and room for a home office

3-bedroom · 1,300–1,600 sf · draft

Three-bedroom family home

$1,700–2,200 / month

A larger two-story timber home for bigger and multi-generational families, on the same car-free lanes as the two-bedroom homes. Three bedrooms, a family kitchen, a bath on each floor and a garden with room to play and grow food. This home type is a draft for Stage 2 design; about 10 are planned in Phase 1.

  • Best for: families with two or three children, and three-generation households
  • Where: ring housing beside the learning pavilions and the food-growing edge
  • Includes: private garden, two baths, extra storage and a flexible ground-floor room that can become a bedroom for a grandparent

How the fee works for a household

  • Home fee: the monthly price of the home. It covers the home, utilities, maintenance and one adult's meals, wellness and programs.
  • Each additional adult: about $300 a month for communal meals, wellness and programs.
  • Each child under 18: about $150 a month. Learning programs on site are included.
  • Example: two parents and two children in a two-bedroom home pay about $1,500 + $300 + $150 + $150 = $2,100 a month.

Families can move to a larger or smaller home as they change, when one is free. Residents already living here get first choice.

Draft. The household fees and size limits above are planning assumptions for Stage 2 (Design, 2027) and will be tested in the waitlist survey before they are final. Local building and occupancy codes at the chosen site take priority.

Beyond the box

Earth tiny homes: built from the ground they stand on.

An option for the tiny-home clusters: small curved homes of earthbag (superadobe) or 3D-printed local soil, finished in clay plaster. Lower cost, healthier air and a quiet, rounded shape.

Earth option · tiny home

Superadobe and printed-earth clusters

Dry and mild climates first

Three to six rounded earth homes share a courtyard with planted beds and a fire circle. Each home keeps the tiny-home layout: a sleeping niche, a compact kitchen and bath, and big windows. The curved walls carry load in compression, so they need little steel or concrete.

  • Earthbag (superadobe): long bags filled with moistened local soil, coiled into domes and vaults. Materials can run about $5–15 per square foot and owner-built homes about $10–50 per square foot (earth.dev).
  • 3D-printed earth: TECLA in Italy was printed from local soil by two coordinated printers (WASP), a model for a printed cluster.

Inside · build together

Clay interiors, built with residents

Pilot in Stage 4

Inside, curved clay walls, an oculus for daylight and built-in benches make a small home feel calm and generous. Earth homes can be built partly by the people who will live in them, with trained leads. Hours on the build can count toward contribution-based residency, lowering monthly costs further.

  • Clay plaster: clay buffers humidity and avoids synthetic finishes, which supports healthier indoor air and soft acoustics.
  • Climate: earth walls suit dry and mild climates best; wet or very cold sites need extra roof overhang and insulation.
  • Permits: earth building codes vary by place, so the site choice in Stage 2 decides which method we use.
  • Learn more: Beyond the Box and the organic forms on the City page.

One fee

What is included, and what is not.

Included in every home

  • Electricity, water, heating, cooling and fast internet
  • 10–15 communal meals a month, cooked from community gardens where possible
  • Sauna, yoga, meditation spaces and wellness programs
  • Building maintenance and shared tools, workshops and vehicles
  • Community events, festivals and learning programs
  • A voice and a vote in community governance

Paid separately

  • Additional meals beyond the included allowance
  • Specialist workshops, retreats and personal services
  • Personal purchases and travel

Because utilities, part of your food, wellness and shared tools are bundled, the fee should be compared with rent plus those costs, not with rent alone. A full cost comparison will be published during Stage 2.

Contribution-based residency

Contribute your time, lower your costs.

About 20–30% of homes are offered to residents who contribute regular work in exchange for reduced fees. A pilot runs in 2027–2028.

Tier 2 · Partial15–20 hrs / week$250–400Studio or tiny home, 10 meals / week, basic healthcare, full votePart-time remote workers, artists, students
Tier 3 · Skill-share8–12 hrs / week$500–700Private studio, 5 meals / week, full voteArchitects, engineers, therapists, teachers

Contribution areas include food systems, facilities and maintenance, education and facilitation, care and support, creative work, and administration. Contribution-based residents have the same rights as paying residents.
