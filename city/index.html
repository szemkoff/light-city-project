The city

A city laid out like a living system.

Concentric rings from a central spire to a wild buffer: gathering at the heart, homes in the middle, food and energy at the edge. Everything is walkable.

The rings

From the center outward.

The plan follows concentric circles joined by radial paths, like spokes. Quiet and shared life sits at the center; production and wild land sit at the edge.

  1. Center

    Spire & plaza

    The landmark tower, the main plaza, markets and festivals.

  2. Inner ring

    Temple & learning

    Temple, quiet rooms, library, learning center and clinic.

  3. Middle ring

    Studios & domes

    Maker studios, co-working and the communosphere domes with their apartments.

  4. Outer ring

    Homes & gardens

    Family ring housing, tiny-home clusters, greenhouses and food forest.

  5. Edge

    Energy & wild buffer

    Solar microgrid, water treatment, fields and a protected woodland margin.

Key structures

What gets built, and how ready it is.

Each structure shows its Technology Readiness Level (TRL): 9 means proven at scale, 1 means early research.

Central sanctum

The spire

TRL 4–5 at full scale

The spire is the city's orientation point: visible from every path, lit at night, and carrying building-integrated solar. Its first purpose is practical: a landmark, an observation deck, a communications mast and a solar collector.

Tall timber-hybrid towers exist today, but a lattice spire of this height with integrated solar has not been built, so it will be engineered and reviewed in Stage 2. Phase 2 could extend it to 120–300 m.

Neighborhood

Communosphere

TRL 7–9

Each communosphere is a neighborhood under one dome: apartments terraced around a planted atrium, a shared kitchen and dining hall, and wellness spaces. The shell creates a mild indoor climate, cutting heating and cooling needs.

Geodesic domes and cross-laminated timber are both proven. The new part is combining them at this size with homes inside, which is a design challenge rather than a research one.

Inner ring

Temple & plaza

TRL 9

A non-denominational temple for silence, meditation and community ceremonies, proportioned with sacred geometry, with a central crystal under the oculus and a fire bowl for evening gatherings. Participation in any practice is always voluntary.

The plaza beside it is the city's living room: morning markets, performances, seasonal festivals and the everyday crossing point of the whole community.

Middle ring

Studios & workshops

TRL 9

Shared studios make it possible to live in a small home and still have access to a full woodshop, ceramics, textiles, digital fabrication and quiet co-working.

They also support the local economy: residents can teach workshops, sell what they make and repair what the city needs.

Outer ring

Greenhouses & food forest

TRL 8–9

Year-round solar greenhouses, aquaponics and a layered food forest supply a meaningful share of fresh produce in Phase 1, growing to 5–10 ha and near self-sufficiency in Phase 2. Compost from kitchens returns to the soil, closing the loop.

Phase 1 is deliberately modest: staple grains and some goods will still come from regional farms, which also builds ties with neighbors.

Edge

Microgrid

TRL 8–9

A community-owned solar microgrid with battery storage powers homes, workshops and greenhouses. It stays connected to the regional grid for resilience and can island during outages.

All of this is proven, off-the-shelf technology. The design work is in sizing, siting and making the energy flows visible to residents.

Beyond the box

Curved, healthy and affordable.

High-tech design, low-tech materials: computers find efficient curved shapes, then we build them from earth, timber, bamboo and lime that residents can handle. Curves use less material, hold heat better and are rated more beautiful in brain-imaging studies.

Tiny homes

Earth homes from local soil

Proven to Emerging

Tiny-home clusters can be built from superadobe (earth-filled bags coiled into domes) or printed from raw earth, as in the TECLA prototype in Italy. Residents can help build, and that work can count toward contribution-based residency.

Earth walls breathe, store heat and contain no synthetic finishes. Durability in wet or freezing climates still needs proof, so we test before we commit.

Studios, domes, greenhouses

Timber gridshells

Proven

Thin wooden laths are laid flat, bent into a double curve and locked in place. They span large rooms with little wood, which suits studios, market halls, greenhouses and the communosphere roofs.

Healthy interiors

Clay plaster everywhere

Holds indoor humidity steady and removes ozone. The cheapest health upgrade, and it works on curves and in existing homes.

Canopies & floors

Thin shells

Arched shells carry load in compression, using over 70% less concrete and allowing earth or recycled mixes.

Practical

Curved shell, efficient core

Kitchens, baths and storage stay straight inside curved rooms, so standard fittings still work and costs stay down.

Frontier

Bio-resonance as research

We will compare matched curved and boxy rooms for sleep, stress and acoustics with university partners, and publish results either way.

Design language

Four rules shape every building.

Proportion

Sacred geometry

Circles, spirals and the golden ratio set the plan, room proportions and facade rhythms, giving the city a coherent, calming order.

Nature

Biophilic design

Daylight, planting, natural materials and views to green in every space. Proven to lower stress and improve wellbeing.

Atmosphere

Light & color

Warm whites, pearl and gold, with lighting tuned to the time of day to support natural sleep and focus rhythms.

Materials

Low-carbon materials

Cross-laminated timber, hempcrete, rammed earth and mycelium insulation store carbon instead of emitting it.
